Writing

The Invention of Writing: Before and After

Before Writing

  • More than 99% of human history falls under this period (~3 million to 5000 years ago).
  • Artefacts (tools and objects) are the primary sources for reconstructing daily life.
  • Understanding human thoughts, ideas, and beliefs remains difficult.
  • Time measurement and event dating are strictly approximate.

After Writing

  • Covers less than 1% of human history (~5000 years ago to present).
  • Combines material remains and written records for accurate reconstruction.
  • Written literature reveals names, historical events, and cultural structures.
  • Dating becomes precise due to documented records like war and coronation dates.

The Harappan Script Enigma

One of the earliest writing systems belongs to the Indus Valley (Harappan) Civilisation.

  • Pictographic Script: Found on seals and pottery across ancient sites.
  • Undeciphered: Known as Sindhu lipi, its meaning remains unknown to modern scholars.

Other Early Global Scripts

Sumerian cuneiform and Egyptian hieroglyphics flourished around the same era as the Harappan Civilisation (~5000 years ago). Both scripts have been successfully deciphered, providing direct insight into early historic periods.

Used from around 400 BCE across southern India and the Ganga Valley, Brahmi was later formalised under Emperor Ashoka in the 3rd century BCE.

Early Chinese writing involved symbols carved onto animal bones and tortoise shells. Heated until cracked, these 'oracles' were read to foretell future events.

The Chinese script is logographic, where each character represents an entire word or concept rather than a phonetic sound.

Egypt

The Gift of the Nile

City-states emerged around 3000 BCE due to expanding populations along the Nile valley.

Kemet: The Black Land

  • Annual summer Nile floods deposited rich, dark silt along the banks.
  • The fertile mud, called kemet, formed the foundation for Egyptian agriculture.
  • The ancient inhabitants named their kingdom Kemet ('the black land') after this soil.

Farming, Administration & Time

The Birth of Administration

Constructing irrigation canals, dams, and reservoirs required large-scale collective action, driving the emergence of local governance and an organized administrative class.

The Egyptian Calendar

Tracking the intervals between Nile floods led to a highly accurate 365-day calendar based on the heliacal rising of Sirius (the Dog Star):

  • Three Seasons: Inundation (Akhet), Growing (Peret), and Harvest (Shemu) — 4 months each.
  • Structure: 12 months of 30 days + 5 additional festival days.

Writing, Papyrus & The Rosetta Stone

Papyrus paper was crafted by layering strips of the plant's inner pith, pressing, and drying them into smooth sheets. Ancient libraries stored these rolled scrolls in labeled jars, preserving medical texts like the Papyrus Ebers, folklore, and administrative records.

Discovered in 1799, the Rosetta Stone featured the same text written in three scripts: Hieroglyphic, Demotic, and Ancient Greek. Using the Greek text as a key, French linguist Jean-François Champollion deciphered ancient hieroglyphics in 1822.

Society, Religion & Pyramids

Social Structure

  1. Pharaoh: Absolute ruler and religious leader
  2. Nobles, Officials & Priests: Administrators and spiritual authorities
  3. Artisans, Merchants & Farmers: Skilled labor and agricultural workforce
  4. Servants & Slaves: Unskilled laborers

Ancient Egyptians believed the ka (spiritual essence) survived physical death if the body remained intact. Mummification involved organ removal, drying with natron salt, wrapping in linen, and performing sacred burial rituals.

Royal tombs evolved from flat-roofed rectangular mud-brick structures (mastabas) into stacked step pyramids (such as Saqqara), eventually progressing to smooth-sided stone pyramids.

Leisure, Festivals & Women's Rights

Leisure & Festivals

  • Recreational activities included board games, swimming, hunting, and musical performances.
  • Religious festivals featured processions using instruments like the sistrum rattle.
  • The Sed Festival was celebrated to renew the Pharaoh's divine authority every 30 years.

Status of Women

Egyptian women held legal rights superior to many neighboring ancient cultures: they could own real estate, execute legal contracts, run businesses, and attain positions of supreme political power (e.g., Cleopatra VII).
