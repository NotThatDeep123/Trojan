Writing

The Invention of Writing: Before and After

Before Writing

  • More than 99 per cent of human history falls under this period (from about 3 million years ago to 5000 years ago)[cite: 1].
  • Tools, implements, and other material objects made by humans (artefacts) are the major sources for reconstructing people's ways of living[cite: 1].
  • It is generally difficult to understand the thoughts and ideas of people[cite: 1].
  • Measurement of time is approximate, meaning dating events and cultures is only approximate[cite: 1].

After Writing

  • Less than 1 per cent of human history falls under this period, the last 5000 years, including the present[cite: 1].
  • Both material remains and written documents are sources for reconstructing lives and society[cite: 1].
  • Literature (written documents) provides information about names, events, and social, political, and cultural life[cite: 1].
  • Dating becomes relatively accurate because written documents mention specific dates of events like coronations and wars[cite: 1].

The Harappan Script Enigma

One of the earliest known writing systems is associated with the Sindhu-Sarasvatī, also known as the Indus Valley or Harappan Civilisation[cite: 1].

  • Pictographic Script: Writings and engravings found on objects such as seals and pottery indicate that the Harappans used a pictographic script[cite: 1].
  • Undeciphered: It has not yet been deciphered[cite: 1]. As a result, we still do not understand the Harappan script, which is also referred to as Sindhu lipi[cite: 1].

Other Early Global Scripts

Other early writing systems include the cuneiform script of the Sumerians in Mesopotamia and the hieroglyphic script of ancient Egypt[cite: 1]. Both flourished around the same time as the Harappan Civilisation[cite: 1]. Unlike the Harappan script, these scripts have been deciphered and mark the beginning of the historical period, about 5000 years ago[cite: 1].

From about 400 BCE, a script known as Brahmi was used in parts of southern India and in the Ganga Valley (northern India)[cite: 1]. It was later formalised by the Emperor Ashoka (3rd century BCE) and contemporary Iron Age people all over southern India[cite: 1].

The earliest source of information about China are the 'oracles', which were symbols made on pieces of animal bones and tortoise shells[cite: 1]. These bones were heated until they cracked, and interpretations were made based on the crack patterns to foretell the future[cite: 1]. They tell us about the hopes, desires, and fears of the early Chinese[cite: 1].

The Chinese script is logographic, with characters representing entire words or morphemes rather than sounds (for example, characters representing a "Person" or a "Tree")[cite: 1].

Egypt

The Gift of the Nile

Egypt saw the emergence of city-states around 3000 BCE as population increased and resources became available[cite: 1].

Kemet: The Black Land

  • The river Nile watered the land along its banks, and every summer the river flooded, leading to annual inundations[cite: 1].
  • This left rich mud, called 'kemet', which was excellent for growing crops[cite: 1].
  • Egyptians referred to their land as Kemet, which means 'the black', coming from the black river valley soil[cite: 1].

Historical Context: Egypt was known to the Greeks and Romans; Greek writers like Herodotus visited and wrote about Egypt in the 5th century BCE, and a 19th-century collection titled de L'Egypte provides fascinating details[cite: 1].

Farming, Administration & Time

The Birth of Administration

About 5000 years ago, farmers discovered that by digging ditches they could divert water from the Nile into their fields and store water in reservoirs for later use[cite: 1]. The need to dig ditches and construct dams required collective effort, which must have led to the growth of local government and the rise of an administrative class[cite: 1].

The Egyptian Calendar

By counting the days between Nile floods, they developed a calendar[cite: 1].

  • Three Seasons: Inundation (autumn), Peret growing (winter), and shemu harvest (summer) - each consisting of four months[cite: 1].
  • The Year: Based on the rising of Sirius (Dog Star)[cite: 1]. Year = 365 days (12 months x 30 days + 5 extra days)[cite: 1].
  • It was quite accurate, but lost time since the real year is about 1/4 day longer[cite: 1].

Writing, Papyrus & The Rosetta Stone

Egyptian history is reconstructed from papyrus or old paper records[cite: 1]. Papyrus was made by cutting the inner trunk of the plant into strips, criss-crossing, pressing, drying, and polishing them into sheets[cite: 1]. (For example, the Papyrus Ebers lists more than 700 cures and spells of Egyptian medicine)[cite: 1].

Libraries: Dating back to 2000 BCE, they stored papyrus scrolls in labeled jars on shelves[cite: 1]. Stories included the oldest version of 'Sindbad the Sailor', animal fables suggesting a link to 'Aesop's Fables', tales of ghosts, miracles, romances, and the oldest known form of 'Cinderella'[cite: 1].

In 1799, Pierre Bouchard, a French army engineer repairing a fort in Egypt, found a giant black stone covered in mysterious writing—the Rosetta Stone[cite: 1].

It had three types of writing, including Greek[cite: 1]. Since people could read Greek, it acted as a bilingual dictionary[cite: 1]. In 1822, a French linguist, Jean-François Champollion, finally deciphered the script[cite: 1].

Society, Religion & Pyramids

Social Hierarchy Pyramid

The society was strictly divided[cite: 1]:

  1. Pharaoh (at the top)
  2. Government Officials, Nobles and Priests
  3. Free Landholders, Artisans and Merchants
  4. Serfs and Slaves (at the bottom)

Egyptians believed that each person had a ka (a spiritual double) that lived on after death if the body was preserved[cite: 1]. The Papyrus of Ani even depicts a scale weighing a heart against a feather to judge the dead[cite: 1].

Mummification process: Involved removing internal organs (except the heart), drying the body with natron, oiling it, wrapping it in linen strips, placing it in a coffin, and burying it with rituals[cite: 1].

Over time, powerful individuals called 'Pharaohs' emerged as rulers[cite: 1]. After death, they were buried deep underground and a rectangular structure called a mastaba was placed over the burial chamber[cite: 1]. Gradually, these mastabas were placed one on top of the other to form a pyramid, such as the step pyramid at Saqqara[cite: 1].

Leisure, Festivals & Women's Rights

Leisure & Festivals

  • Early Egyptians enjoyed leisure activities: they swam, canoed, played board games, and enjoyed music and dancing[cite: 1].
  • Festivals were central, dedicated to gods and the pharaoh's rule[cite: 1]. Statues of gods were paraded accompanied by music played on instruments like the 'sistrum'[cite: 1].
  • The Sed festival was held to mark a king's 30th year on the throne[cite: 1].

Women's Rights

Egyptian women, in general, enjoyed more rights than their Greek or Roman counterparts, as they could own property and run businesses[cite: 1]. For instance, Cleopatra (69-30 BCE) was trained from childhood to rule and became queen at the age of eighteen[cite: 1].
